Non-penetrating World Models

AbstractRead

We study a new problem of preventing object penetration in interactive video world models. To understand how existing models handle this, we introduce NoPeBench, a benchmark that evaluates how world models respond to physical obstacles. Our evaluation reveals that none of the open-source models can reliably prevent such failures.

Surprisingly, however, these models already encode the information needed to predict such violations. We find that the hidden states of diffusion transformers (DiTs) can predict whether a candidate action will penetrate an obstacle. Building on this, we introduce NoPeWorld, a lightweight framework that detects and prevents penetration. Specifically, we train a small prediction head on top of a frozen DiT's hidden states to read out if a candidate action is safe or not. Unsafe actions are then replaced with safe ones that best preserve the original intent.

Despite its simplicity, NoPeWorld substantially reduces the physical violation rate, averaged over six video world models, from 74% to 16%. Remarkably, our method shows zero-shot generalization to open-domain imagery, third-person viewpoints, and moving obstacles. We publicly release our code, the NoPeBench benchmark, and model weights at the links above.

03Method

Read the DiT, replace unsafe actions.

Before each chunk, a head under 2 M parameters scores 34 candidate motions on the frozen DiT: a safe input is kept, an unsafe one becomes the closest safe motion.

  1. 1Read. A head under 2 M parameters reads the frozen DiT before the chunk is drawn.
  2. 2Score. It rates 34 candidate motions for safety and intent.
  3. 3Act. Safe: keep the input. Unsafe: take the closest safe motion.
