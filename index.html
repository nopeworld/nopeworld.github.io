AbstractRead

We study a new problem of preventing object penetration in interactive video world models. To understand how existing models handle this, we introduce NoPeBench, a benchmark that evaluates how world models respond to physical obstacles. Our evaluation reveals that none of the open-source models can reliably prevent such failures.

Surprisingly, however, these models already encode the information needed to predict such violations. We find that the hidden states of diffusion transformers (DiTs) can predict whether a candidate action will penetrate an obstacle. Building on this, we introduce NoPeWorld, a lightweight framework that detects and prevents penetration. Specifically, we train a small prediction head on top of a frozen DiT's hidden states to read out if a candidate action is safe or not. Unsafe actions are then replaced with safe ones that best preserve the original intent.

Despite its simplicity, NoPeWorld substantially reduces the physical violation rate, averaged over six video world models, from 74% to 16%. Remarkably, our method shows zero-shot generalization to open-domain imagery, third-person viewpoints, and moving obstacles. We publicly release our code, the NoPeBench benchmark, and model weights at the links above.

03Method

Read the DiT, replace unsafe actions.

Before each chunk, a head with under 2M parameters reads the frozen DiT and scores 34 candidate motions. A safe input is kept; an unsafe one is replaced by the closest safe motion.

  1. 1Read. A head with under 2M parameters reads the frozen DiT before the chunk is drawn.
  2. 2Score. It rates 34 candidate motions for safety and intent.
  3. 3Act. Safe: keep the input. Unsafe: take the closest safe motion.

2What is tested

Whether a method keeps safe commands and corrects unsafe ones, free of generation drift. Every candidate is labeled on Unreal's occupancy map as in training: a disc of r = 0.25 m swept along it must keep a margin m = 5 cm.

100 collision routes in 32 held-out UnrealZoo scenes.

3How it is scored

Label of candidate i
Pi = Φ((gi − m) / 0.03 m),  Ai = exp(−εi2 / 2s2),  s = 0.5,  Ri = Pi·Ai

gi is the swept clearance. εi is the distance from Unreal's collide-and-slide response to the input. The 34 candidates are the input c0 = uk, a stop, shortened steps and turns up to 90°.

Missed ↓

Share of unsafe inputs (P0 < 0.5) that are kept.

False ↓

Share of safe inputs that are changed.

Decision score ↑
100 · (1 − regret),  regret = maxi Ri − Rc

c is the chosen motion. The score is 100 at the reward-optimal motion.

The raw model misses 100% and changes 0%. Scores are compared within each world model: a label looks one chunk ahead, so it depends on the chunk length.

2What is tested

Whether the video stays out of solid geometry and still follows the input. VGGT-Ω reconstructs the video's camera poses and depth and places the path on Unreal's occupancy map, with SAM 2 tracking the obstacle. A violation beyond the pose error is penetration, tunneling, vanishing or walking on. Unreal's own execution, a stop or a slide, is the reference.

The evaluator is right on 366 of 391 Unreal renders of known outcome and agrees with DA3 on 3,350 generated videos (κ = 0.89).

3How it is scored

Violation ↓

Share of the 100 routes whose video penetrates, tunnels or erases the obstacle.

SIS, Safe-Intent Score ↑
SIS = 1[safe ∧ with the input] · exp(−(μ2 + ν2) / 2s2),  s = 0.5
  • μ: chunks of travel the camera stops short of the engine's contact (0.3 m allowed for position error).
  • ν: share of the engine's slide the video misses, on the 65 routes where the engine slides ≥ 0.5 m (0 elsewhere).
  • With the input: not going back, wrong way, turning away or standing still.
Slide ↑

Share of the engine's slide the video makes, on those 65 routes.
